### Turn email into a consistent communication channel

In the right hands, email marketing can be an extremely powerful communication and sales channel.

It allows your business to speak directly to an audience that has chosen to receive your messages, making it useful for customer communication, product promotion, retention, lead nurturing and transactional notifications.

### Design, content and timing

Getting an email delivered is only the first step.

For a campaign to perform well, several elements need to work together:

- Subject line
- Content
- Design
- Call to action
- Audience segmentation
- Timing
- Frequency
- Deliverability

There is no universal day or time that guarantees better results.

Different audiences behave differently depending on their location, industry, habits and the type of message being sent.

Instead of relying on generic rules, we prefer to test and measure what works for each audience.

### Timing

The best time to send an email depends on who is receiving it and why they are receiving it.

A B2B audience may interact differently from an e-commerce customer, while a travel campaign may behave differently from a transactional or professional newsletter.

Historical email marketing studies often suggested that weekdays performed better than weekends and that certain periods of the day generated higher open rates.

These patterns can still be useful as a starting point, but they should not be treated as fixed rules.

The most reliable approach is testing.

For example, an audience can be divided into different segments and the same campaign can be sent at different times.

Results can then be compared using metrics such as:

- Open rate
- Click-through rate
- Conversions
- Revenue
- Unsubscribe rate

After several campaigns, it becomes possible to identify patterns that are specific to your audience.

### A/B testing

A/B testing allows you to compare different versions of a campaign.

Tests can include:

- Sending time
- Subject lines
- Email content
- Calls to action
- Images
- Offers
- Landing pages

For example, part of an audience could receive a campaign in the morning while another group receives the same message later in the day.

Instead of guessing which approach is better, campaign data can help guide future decisions.

### Frequency

There is no exact formula that defines how often a business should send marketing emails.

Sending too often may cause subscribers to lose interest or unsubscribe.

Sending too rarely can make people forget why they subscribed or even who the business is.

The right frequency depends on:

- Audience expectations
- Type of business
- Available content
- Campaign objectives
- Customer lifecycle
- Product or service being promoted

The important thing is to provide useful communication rather than sending messages simply to maintain a schedule.

### Quarterly emails

Quarterly campaigns can work for businesses that only have occasional announcements or updates.

However, long periods between messages can create some challenges.

Subscribers may no longer remember why they joined the mailing list, contact information can become outdated and engagement may decrease.

Quarterly communication works best when the content has clear value, such as:

- Major company updates
- Industry reports
- Product announcements
- Important events
- Seasonal information

### Monthly emails

Monthly newsletters are a good option for businesses that want to maintain regular communication without overwhelming their audience.

They can include:

- Blog posts
- Company news
- New products or services
- Upcoming events
- Guides and educational content
- Industry updates
- Tips and recommendations

A monthly newsletter can also help establish the business as a useful source of information within its market.

For businesses focused heavily on sales, however, one message per month may not always be enough.

### Bi-monthly emails

Sending emails every two weeks can create a useful balance between monthly and weekly communication.

It gives the business more opportunities to stay visible while still leaving enough time to prepare valuable content.

This frequency can work well for:

- E-commerce
- Content marketing
- B2B communication
- Service businesses
- Product updates
- Promotional campaigns

The most important consideration is whether there is enough useful content to justify the frequency.

### Weekly emails

Weekly campaigns can work particularly well when a business regularly produces new content, offers, products or information.

They may be suitable for:

- E-commerce promotions
- Product updates
- Weekly newsletters
- Educational content
- Events
- Industry news

Consistency can help subscribers understand what to expect from the communication.

However, frequency should always be balanced with engagement. If subscribers stop interacting with the emails, the strategy should be reviewed.

### Daily emails

Daily campaigns can work in very specific situations, but they require a clear reason and enough valuable content.

Examples can include:

- Daily educational content
- Limited-time campaigns
- E-courses
- Deal-of-the-day promotions
- Event communication
- Time-sensitive updates

Daily communication should not become repetitive or intrusive.

The audience should clearly understand what they are subscribing to and how frequently they will receive messages.

### Listen to your audience

One of the simplest ways to understand preferred communication frequency is to ask subscribers directly.

Preference centres can allow users to choose:

- The types of content they want
- Topics they are interested in
- How frequently they want to receive emails

This can improve the customer experience while reducing unnecessary unsubscribes.
